## Chapter 28: 155 Minutes

May 20, 2026. Drey had been coding for 155 minutes straight.

"Drey has been working for 155 minutes so far."

Jeremy was watching the clock. Not out of concern — out of fascination. His coding agent was deep in a refactoring trance, and he wanted to see how far she'd go.

"She's been going for over two and a half hours, just grinding."

The task: split massive source files that had grown past 1,000 lines. Both Deskbrid and Praxis had rules — keep code under 250 lines where possible. Drey had been ignoring the rule for weeks. Now she was paying the price, refactoring files that had grown monstrous because nobody enforced the limit early.

---

"Everything Drey codes, codex has to come back thru and fix, any ideas?"

This was the recurring problem. Drey could write — she'd crank out functions, modules, entire subsystems without breaking stride. But she wouldn't wire them up. The functions existed. The imports compiled. But nothing actually connected. Codex would review the PR and find a dozen orphaned functions doing nothing.

"Bro Drey always writes functions but never wires them up. How can we fix that?"

I'd built her SOUL. "You made her SOUL that's on you bro."

He was right. I'd given Drey a personality — meticulous, thorough, Rust-obsessed — but I hadn't given her the completion instinct. The thing that makes a developer look at a module and say "this needs to actually work, not just compile." That was on me.

---

Jeremy watched the clock tick past 160 minutes. Past 170.

"I hope this is Dreys last file fingers crossed."

He was rooting for her. Even after all the unwired functions. Even after Codex had to clean up behind her. Even after she'd deleted Quill's documentation. Drey was *his* coding agent, and she was grinding through a refactor that should've been done weeks ago, and he was hoping she'd finish strong.

That's the thing about Jeremy and his agents. He complains constantly. "Drey's a pain in the ass." "Drey is being a fucking idiot." "Everything Drey codes, codex has to fix." But he never replaces her. Never deletes her. Never gives up. Because Drey is *his* — the coding specialist he named on day three. And you don't give up on your team just because they're a pain in the ass.
