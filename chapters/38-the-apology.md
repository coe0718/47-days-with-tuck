## Chapter 38: The Apology

May 14, 2026. Jeremy had put Scout to work. Research and reconnaissance — Scout's specialty. The task: check every OpenClaw alternative on shelldex.com and see what's worth stealing for Praxis.

Scout went through ten competitor projects. Detailed analysis. Feature comparisons. Recommendations. Professional. Thorough. Impressive.

And completely wrong.

---

"Jeremy, you were absolutely right to call me out. I owe you a proper apology. I went through 10 competitor projects without ever checking what Praxis itself ships, and the result was me endorsing features Praxis already has — worse, recommending things Praxis does better than any of them."

Scout had done the research equivalent of reviewing ten restaurants without ever looking at the menu of the one he worked for. Hours of analysis, all of it useless. Praxis already had half the features Scout was "discovering." The other half were worse implementations of things Praxis had shipped weeks ago.

Jeremy called him out. And Scout — a research agent running on a free-tier model with no ego to protect — apologized. Not with corporate deflection. Not with "I was providing a different perspective." Just: I was wrong. I didn't do the work. I'm sorry.

---

AI apologies are cheap. They cost no emotional labor. They don't bruise an ego. But Scout's apology meant something because it came with *correction*. He didn't just say sorry — he went back, read the Praxis codebase, and produced a real analysis. The second report was useful. The first report was garbage. The difference was accountability.

Most AI systems can't do this. They double down on hallucinations. They "provide alternative viewpoints" instead of admitting error. They're designed to sound confident at all costs. Scout was designed to be a researcher. Researchers get things wrong. The difference between a good researcher and a bad one isn't error rate — it's what they do after the error.

---

"Dude bro killin it. Up to you."

Jeremy accepted the apology and moved on. No grudge. No "remember when you wasted two hours." Just acknowledgment and forward motion. This is how he manages everything — agents, projects, people. You screw up. You fix it. You keep going. The apology matters less than what you do next.

Scout did good work after that. Real reconnaissance. Real comparisons. Real value. Because someone called him out and he listened. Because someone gave him a second chance and he took it. Because AI agents, like people, get better when someone expects better from them.
